# Reflection levels with bounded and unbounded gaps

Status: two proved special cases and one open generalization.

## Definitions

`IsReflectionLevel` records a finite reflection symmetry of a covering set at
a level. A cofinal sequence of such levels provides translations between
representations at different scales.

The objective is to construct an infinite deletion while retaining an
order-three basis.

## Bounded-gap case

`MirrorPeriodicity.lean` proves that a cofinal sequence of reflection levels
with bounded successive gaps yields eventual periodic structure. The theorem
`surviving_deletion_of_boundedGap_reflectionLevels` then constructs a
surviving infinite deletion.

This is a proved conditional result. It does not apply when the gaps between
reflection levels are unbounded.

## Geometric-gap case

`UnboundedMirrorGaps.lean` proves
`surviving_deletion_of_geometric_reflectionLevels`. Its hypotheses include:

- the reflection levels are members of the covering set;
- each level is more than twice the preceding level;
- there is an anchor below the first level with a suitable nontrivial
  two-term representation.

The proof deletes alternating reflected copies of the anchor. The geometric
growth separates the possible collisions, and the anchor representation
repairs the case in which both original summands are deleted.

This theorem covers one unbounded-gap regime but not arbitrary spacing.

## Computational example

Finite Cantor-type sets were generated by

`T₀ = [0,b]`,

`Tₙ₊₁ = Tₙ ∪ (3Mₙ - Tₙ)`,

with `Mₙ₊₁ = 3Mₙ`.

The tested truncations have the expected reflection levels and unbounded
geometric gaps. Sparse random deletions and deletion of sampled level maxima
preserved the tested coverage range. These are finite observations only.

## Open generalization

The following statement is open in this repository:

> If an order-two covering set has cofinally many reflection levels, then it
> admits an infinite deletion that is an order-three basis, without any bound
> or geometric condition on the level gaps.

The missing issue is uniform repair when a representation lies near the upper
part of a reflection interval. A translation available at one level may
collide with the deletion chosen at another level. The bounded-gap proof uses
periodicity to control this interaction, while the geometric proof uses scale
separation. Neither argument currently handles arbitrary gaps.

## Relation to the main proof

Reflection theorems provide positive special cases and useful countermodels.
The current general-order proof does not reduce all remaining cases to
reflection levels, so these results do not settle Erdős 881.
