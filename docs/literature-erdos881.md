# Literature check: Erdős problem 881 (2026-07-24)

## The problem, verbatim (erdosproblems.com/881, attribution [Er98])

> OPEN. Let A ⊂ ℕ be an additive basis of order k which is minimal, in
> the sense that if B ⊂ A is any infinite set then A∖B is not a basis of
> order k. Must there exist an infinite B ⊂ A such that A∖B is a basis
> of order k+1?

Source: Erdős, "Some of my new and almost new problems and results in
combinatorial number theory" (Eger 1996 / de Gruyter 1998). The
hypothesis class is the **ℵ₀-minimal bases** of Erdős–Nathanson (Proc.
AMS 53 (1975) 253–258, Thm 2: they exist for order 2; existence for
order ≥ 3 still open per Nathanson 2008, Problem 16). Also formalized in
google-deepmind/formal-conjectures (`ErdosProblems/881.lean`).

**Forum note (unverified):** a May 2026 forum post claims YES for k=1
and **NO for every k ≥ 2** via thin sets A(x) = O(x^{1/k}); Thomas Bloom
noted the proof "seems entirely generated by GPT" and an independent
check "claimed several major gaps". Problem remains marked OPEN. The
claim leans opposite to this repo's experimental lean — thin
(Sidon-like) bases are the territory it uses, and our own small-guardian
discovery (sparse symmetric sets) points the same direction. Treat thin
bases as the serious counterexample zone.

## What is known that touches our theorems

- **Finiteness of singleton order-3 destroyers is KNOWN.**
  Erdős–Graham, "On bases with an exact order" (Acta Arith. 37 (1980)):
  finitely many essential elements. Grekos (Bordeaux 1987–88): #essential
  ≤ E(h). **Cassaigne–Plagne, Proc. AMS 132 (2004) 2833–2840: S(2)=3** —
  in any exact asymptotic basis of order 2, all but finitely many a have
  A∖{a} an exact basis of order ≤ 3. This is the singleton-deletion case
  of 881 (k=2), solved. Our `no_big_guardian_stacking` proves a
  consequence in this circle by a different (two-scale contradiction)
  route; headline credit belongs to Erdős–Graham/Cassaigne–Plagne.
  E(h) asymptotics: Deschamps–Farhi 2007, Plagne 2008 (~2√(h/log h)).
- **The unique level-1 shape appears in Erdős–Nathanson 1975**: their
  A_Q construction uses interval blocks whose non-represented targets
  are exactly 2q_k + 1 — the "[0,M] ∪ {2M+1} guards 3M+1" shape our
  exhaustive search found. Modular-interval extremal examples also
  pervade Plagne's X-function paper (Ann. Inst. Fourier 54 (2004)).
- **Not found anywhere**: (a) mirror/palindromic *rigidity* theorems for
  destroyers (our desert/mirror/boundary chain appears novel as stated —
  but the unread Cassaigne–Plagne proof may contain overlapping
  structural lemmas; read it before claiming priority); (c) any
  surviving-deletion theorem for periodic / geometric-mirror /
  cofinal-mirror bases — **our `MirrorPeriodicity` +
  `UnboundedMirrorGaps` chain appears to be the first positive partial
  case of 881 for k=2** beyond trivialities.
- **No published progress on 881 itself** (searches over arXiv and the
  problem site found none). The 1979 Erdős–Nathanson SDR paper and the
  1988 partitions-of-bases paper both assume representation-count growth
  incompatible with minimal bases, so they do not touch 881.

## Cassaigne–Plagne, read first-hand (2026-07-24, AMS PDF via curl + browser UA)

Overlap verdict, now confirmed directly:
- Their Theorem 1 (S(2)=3) finishes with a **two-destroyer contradiction**
  (two elements b > b' with cofinal private targets; a forced element
  d = c - b - b' plus e = c - b makes d + b' = e violate the privacy of
  b') — the same *kind* of two-scale clash as `no_big_guardian_stacking`,
  but executed through order-exactness bookkeeping (their Lemmas 1–3),
  not through desert/mirror geometry. Our rigidity *statements* (mirror
  symmetry, deserts, the [0,M] ∪ {2M+1} shape, forced-translate windows)
  do not appear there; our no-stacking headline is a known consequence
  with a different proof.
- **Imported technique (their Lemma 4):** for a basis of exact order h,
  the densities δ(a) of "targets needing a" satisfy Σ_a δ(a) ≤ h — a
  one-line double-count (each target needs ≤ h elements). A pair
  version needs care (a singleton-guarded target is covered by unboundedly
  many pairs through its guardian): for targets with two DISJOINT
  supports, covering pairs live in S × S', so ≤ 9 per target; the
  singleton-guarded and intersecting-family cases must be split off
  first. With that split, team-edge densities for genuinely pair-guarded
  targets are summable — a quantitative route toward Open Link B, for
  the *cofinally-dense* team graph (single-event edges are not
  constrained by density; closing that gap is part of Link B).
